The lab's coAdapt experiment, run with realistic voices

coAdapt (simonMulti, Experiment 1) is one of the blab lab's public formant-perturbation experiments. A participant reads three words aloud, trial after trial. For two of the words Audapter moves the first formant (F1) of what they hear in their headphones, one word up and one down, rising to 125 mel during the ramp phase and holding there; the third word is never shifted. Which word gets which shift is counterbalanced across participants.

Here the lab's own script ran unmodified, in its test mode, on a simulated rig with the real Audapter core, and a virtual participant said each word the script put on screen (the report's methods describe how). Talkers marked AI-generated are voices from OpenAI's gpt-audio-1.5 speech model saying just the requested word: realistic renditions, not recordings of people, with no audio above 12 kHz. Choose a talker.

The figures use the report's colours: dashed ink is what the experiment intends (expected), blue is what actually happened (observed: the shift from Audapter's own log, the level measured on the audio), and orange marks where the two differ. Levels are RMS; the report's FMT-LEVEL card estimates how much of a level difference like this a listener perceives.

Preview, results provisional. The simulated participants here use the default LPC order for their male/female setting; a simulated LPC check (see how the LPC order was chosen) is being added and may change the order for some voices. The independent formant check is shown where it is available.

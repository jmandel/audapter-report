# HTML report for the blab-lab maintainers: format and plan

Status: the full report is built from this plan (see section 13, "Build status"). Everything in it is generated by `build.py`
from `findings.yaml`, `cards/*.yaml` and real harness or live-path runs. Nothing is hand-drawn.

## 1. Audience, goal, tone

- **Readers**: blab-lab speech-motor-control researchers and their lab programmer. They know Audapter well
  as users and maintainers (OST/PCF files, fb modes, `AudapterIO`, `getData` fields). They know nothing about this audit.
- **They need to know within minutes**: what we looked at and why, how we ran it without MATLAB or a sound card, what
  is wrong in terms of *experimental effect*, and why they can trust it. Only after that do they need the code.
- **Tone**: factual and collegial. We report to maintainers; we are not grading them. Say what is solid first, and
  say it specifically (bit-exact differential, formant shifts deliver what they log, pitch accurate to 2 cents).
  Mark our own uncertainty and judgement (severity) as ours. Frame blab-intended behaviour changes (F6 dropout fix) as questions.
- **Words**: plain verbs, sentence case, units with non-breaking spaces, the lab's own vocabulary (state, trial,
  `ost_stat`, `sfmts`, fb 3). No "critical vulnerability" register.

## 2. Information architecture

A single long page with a sticky-free, linkable structure. Every section and card has a stable `id`. The order is fixed:

1. **Masthead**: title, a one-paragraph summary of what we did, pinned SHAs (reviewed and baseline), and the date.
2. **In brief** (about 2 paragraphs): what is solid, then the kinds of problems found, linking the top 3–4 cards. No hero number.
3. **What we looked at, and why**: scope (C++ core in 4 areas, MATLAB wrapper), blab-only diff first, silent bugs first.
4. **How we ran Audapter without MATLAB or a sound card**: a numbered 5-step chain (it is a real sequence),
   the three builds, the portability patches, and the verification-label legend (H, D, R, F).
5. **Controls that ruled things out** (trust-builders): the blab-vs-upstream differential table (rendered from
   `logs-summary.txt`), NEG-2 (the F1 "overshoot" was estimator bias), NEG-1 ("diverging trials" was a harness artifact
   that exposed I-04), and a short list of things checked and found correct.
6. **Limitations**: Octave vs MATLAB, offline path only, GCC vs MSVC heap, synthetic speech, severity is our judgement.
7. **Reading the finding cards**: the four visual encodings, what the audio normalisation means, and what
   "inherited" and "blab-amplified" mean.
8. **Findings at a glance**: one ranked table of every finding (ID, severity glyph + word, one-line effect, origin,
   verification). Rows with cards link to them.
9. **Finding cards**, in rank order (section 3).
10. **Short cards**: the second tier, with effect, cause and a permalink but no audio (section 11).
11. **Formal analysis** (reserved; `audit/formal/`): what was model-checked or proved, and per-card `F` badges.
12. **Audapter in the browser** (reserved; `audit/wasm/`): the WASM build, its equivalence evidence (native vs WASM
    `equiv-*.json`, max |diff| about 3e-11) and the live demo. Per-card interactive panels are described in section 6.
13. **Reproducing everything**: commands and the repo layout. **Appendix**: every NEG and RETRACTED entry from FINDINGS-LOG.md.

## 4. Visual language for sketches

One visual grammar for every finding, drawn from data by `build.py` (inline SVG, no chart library):

- **Tiers on a shared time axis**, Praat-TextGrid style. Every lab member reads TextGrids. Labels sit in a
  left column (tier name plus a small sub-label such as "first trial" / "after trial A"), and interval boundaries are vertical hairlines. There is
  one time axis per figure, in seconds, from the start of the trial. Insets get their own axis (e.g. the 9.90–10.06 s babble zoom).
- **Four fixed encodings** (validated with the dataviz palette validator, light and dark):
  - *Input*: grey filled envelope (`#aab2bb`).
  - *Expected* (config intent or a control run): hollow interval with an ink outline; a thin ink line for continuous traces.
  - *Observed* (what Audapter did or logged): solid blue (`#2a78d6`, dark `#3987e5`); white labels inside intervals.
  - *Discrepancy*: a translucent orange band (`#eb6834`) spanning exactly the deviation, labelled in **ink** (text never
    takes the series colour). Orange also marks highlighted code lines, the same "this is where it goes wrong" cue.
  - Context (previous trial, OST states for reference): pale grey fill.
- **Continuous values** (F0, level) as line tiers with 2–3 hairline gridlines labelled inside the plot. There is never a
  second y-axis. Level is plotted *relative to a reference run* (e.g. `bPitchShift = 0`), so the expected line is 0 dB.
- **Identity is never colour alone**: sub-labels, direct labels, the shape (circle /a/, square /i/) plus a legend in the sweep chart.
- **Every figure has** an SVG `<title>`/`<desc>` generated from the same numbers, `<title>` tooltips on marks, a
  "Numbers behind this figure" table, and a link to `data.json`.
- **Playhead**: playing a clip draws a vertical line on the card's figure at the clip's time (clip `data-offset` for excerpts).
- Sketch types needed across the full report: `state-timeline` (OST-F1/F2/F8, PT-3), `presence-timeline` (I-01, I-03),
  `trace + reference` (PT-5, I-02 level ratio, F6 F1 trajectory with perturbation-on tier), `waveform inset` (I-01
  dropout, PT-2), `small dot plot` (sweeps), and `memory` (OST-F4, FMT-F1: array extent vs read extent, with the ASan line quoted).

## 5. Audio assets

- **Produced by** `harness/oct/report_<finding>.m` export scripts, run with `./run-oct.sh` (and with
  `VARIANT=upstream … build-upstream upstream/audapter_matlab` to confirm origin). Clips are Audapter's own `signalIn` / `signalOut`
  (16 kHz), so they are exactly what was recorded, not a re-synthesis. Written to `harness/oct/out/report/<id>/<build>/`, with a `data.json`
  holding every number and trace the card shows.
- **Loudness**: `report_wavgroup.m` applies **one shared gain per card**. The loudest clip's active RMS (20 ms blocks
  above 1e-3) goes to −20 dBFS, capped so that no clip peaks above −1 dBFS. Level differences inside a card are therefore real. This matters for PT-5,
  where the level difference *is* the finding. The gain and each clip's RMS and peak go into data.json.
- **Warnings**: each clip can carry a `warn` string, rendered beside the player (abrupt stop, 16 ms dropout, and in future
  the time-domain shifter's +21 dB burst). The −1 dBFS peak cap lowers the whole group's gain rather than clipping. Planned: flag automatically any clip whose peak is more than 20 dB above its active RMS.
- **Formats and storage**: WAV 16-bit is canonical, kept in `prototype/assets/<id>/`. The 3 prototype cards use 1.36 MB of WAV.
  For about 12 full cards expect about 5–6 MB of WAV, or about 1.5 MB as Opus 48 kb/s (a 12 s clip is 384 KB WAV, 195 KB FLAC, 74 KB Opus).
- **Embedding**: two outputs from one build.
  - *Directory build* (`index.html` + `assets/`): `<audio preload="none" src=…>`, the lightest option, and the default for hosting or a zip.
  - *Single file* (`build.py --single` → `index.single.html`): audio inlined as data URIs, currently 1.8 MB for 3 cards.
    For the full report use FLAC (lossless, supported in all current browsers) in the single file to keep it under about 8 MB,
    or Opus if we accept lossy (fine for listening, not for analysis; say so in the page).
- Device-rate (48 kHz) inputs are also exported, unscaled, for the in-browser panels.

## 6. In-browser panels (WASM), as progressive enhancement

The static card is complete without JavaScript or WASM: sketch, pre-rendered WAVs, code, verification. The panel
sits between **Listen** and **Cause** (`<section class="interactive">`). Cards without a working panel show a dashed
"planned" box describing it (I-01, PT-5 in the prototype).

- **Working now (OST-F1)**: "Run trials B, A, B on both builds" runs the same inputs and parameters as the harness through
  the real core compiled to WASM, as released and patched. It draws the resulting state tiers in the same visual language and plays either output.
  It reproduces the harness onsets to the frame (0.504 / 1.700 / 1.722 s), and the patched build gives 0.504 s after trial A
  (checked by `node report/wasm/check-ost-f1.mjs` and, in Chromium, by `check-render.mjs`; both can run in CI). It took about 330 ms in headless Chromium.
- **Planned panels**: I-01 (choose the datapb length, 1–10 s, and hear where the noise stops; released vs "wrap at loaded length"); PT-5 (move the
  step size and onset; bundled vowel or, over https, your own microphone via the WASM line's AudioWorklet; released vs normalised
  overlap-add); OST-F2 (maxIOI timeout over 3 trials); I-02 (fb 5 with `dScale` 1 vs 2). Mic input is optional and never
  needed to see the bug.
- **Loading**: panels load on click from *classic* `<script>` bundles (`wasm/audapter-variants.js`, `assets/<id>/widget-data.js`),
  which also work from `file://` and offline, where `fetch()` and module imports are blocked. The emscripten module is instantiated from a
  precompiled `WebAssembly.Module` via `instantiateWasm`. The in-page wrapper talks to the C API (`aud_*`) directly, so it does not
  depend on the moving JS API in `audit/wasm/web`.
- **Pinning**: variants are built from `git archive <pinned blab SHA>`, never from a working tree, with the same `prep-src.sh`
  portability edits as the native harness. `build-info.json` records the SHA, the variant and the patch hashes. The C API is a vendored snapshot
  (`report/wasm/vendor`, with `SNAPSHOT.sha256`). Once `audit/wasm` publishes stable releases (its `lib/audapter-<v>.js`
  single-file bundles, with `aud_variant()` and `aud_patches()`), switch to consuming those with their manifest and drop our copy.
- **Patched variants**: `report/patches/*.patch`, one small reviewable patch per finding (the prototype has `ost-f1-reset.patch`,
  1 line). These are also what we would offer upstream. Open question 9: a single combined "patched" build (what `audit/wasm` builds: OST-F1,
  OST-F2, I-01) or one build per finding. Combined is cheaper, but each panel must then state that the other fixes cannot affect its scenario.
- **Build variant caveat**: the "lite" build shrinks recorder and playback buffers to fit browser memory (about 140 MB vs 266 MB). That is harmless
  for OST-F1 and PT-5, but **it changes I-01 and I-03**, whose bugs depend on the buffer size. Those panels need the full-size build (or a lite build with only
  the recorder shrunk). Planned: each panel declares the variant it needs, and build.py refuses a mismatch.
- **Size budget**: each WASM variant is 282 KB (about 375 KB as base64 in a classic bundle, about 100 KB gzip). The glue is 66 KB. With 2 variants the
  bundle is 0.9 MB. Inputs are PCM16 48 kHz (192 KB per 2 s, 0.5 MB base64 per panel). Budget: **≤ 1 MB per panel, ≤ 4 MB for all panels**, loaded
  only on click. The static page (HTML + fonts) stays under 300 KB. If panels multiply, send the inputs at 16 kHz and upsample in JS (a third of the size).
- **Failure modes are explicit**: no WebAssembly, or a load error, leaves a one-line status explaining that the static figure and recordings are unaffected.

## 7. Permalink conventions

- Always `https://github.com/<owner>/<repo>/blob/<FULL 40-char SHA>/<path>#L<a>-L<b>` (`#L<a>` for one line) at the SHAs pinned in
  FINDINGS-LOG.md: blab mex `169cadf…`, blab matlab `e1695f1…`, upstream mex `f547196…`. Never a branch name.
- Link text: `<who> · <path> lines a–b @<7-char SHA>`, where who is `blab`, `blab MATLAB` or `upstream`. The compact form is used for the upstream refs.
- The **excerpt comes from the same SHA** (`git show SHA:path`), dedented, with line numbers and highlighted lines. Each ref carries an `expect`
  string that must occur in the excerpt, or the build fails. This guards against line-number drift.
- Blab-only and blab-amplified findings also link the introducing commit or PR (e.g. `commit/3c90a3a…` for the 480000 bump), using the full SHA.
- Harness files are referenced by repo-relative path (`audit/harness/oct/t_ost.m`). These become permalinks once the audit repo is published (open question 3).

## 8. Accessibility, print, offline

- Semantic structure (one `h1`, sections with `h2`, cards as `article` with `aria-labelledby`), a skip link,
  visible `:focus-visible`, native `<audio controls>` (keyboard-operable), copy buttons as real `<button>`s.
- Figures: `role="img"` with generated `<title>`/`<desc>` that state the result in words, plus the numbers table. Colour is never the only
  cue (labels, sub-labels, shapes, glyph + word for severity). Contrast: ink on paper at 12:1 or better; series colours pass the 3:1 mark contrast.
  Validator runs are recorded in the style.css header.
- `prefers-color-scheme: dark` with separately chosen dark steps. `forced-colors` falls back to outline and Highlight bands.
  There is no motion apart from the user-started playhead.
- Small screens: the rail stacks on top, blocks go to one column, and figures keep a 720 px minimum width and scroll horizontally, so
  labels are never squeezed.
- **Print/PDF**: each card starts on a new page. Figures and code blocks don't split. Audio players are hidden, and the clip label
  prints its file name. `beforeprint` opens every "Numbers behind this figure" table. Interactive buttons are hidden. Links are left
  unexpanded to keep code lines clean; the permalink text already shows file, lines and SHA.
- **Offline**: no network requests at all. Fonts (Charis SIL, OFL, a Charter derivative, Latin subset, 92 KB) are inlined as data URIs.
  Scripts are inline. Audio is relative files or inlined in the single-file build. WASM panels use classic-script bundles that work from `file://`.

## 9. Build pipeline

```
harness/oct/report_<id>.m  --run-oct.sh (blab, upstream)-->  harness/oct/out/report/<id>/<build>/{*.wav, data.json}
report/findings.yaml  ---------------------------------------+
FINDINGS-LOG.md (ID check)  ---------------------------------+--> report/build.py --> prototype/index.html (+ assets/, wasm/)
harness/logs-summary.txt (live PASS/FAIL per test)  ---------+                    --> index.single.html (--single)
pinned git SHAs (git show: excerpts + permalinks)  ----------+                    --> build-manifest.json
report/wasm/build-variants.sh (+ patches/*.patch)  --> prototype/wasm/audapter-{buggy,patched}.{mjs,wasm}
report/check-render.mjs (Playwright + Chromium): run panels, screenshots (desktop, mobile, dark), print PDF, console errors
report/wasm/check-ost-f1.mjs (node): WASM variants reproduce harness numbers; patch fixes it
```

- `python3 report/build.py --export` re-runs every export script in docker, then renders. `--single` adds the inlined file.
- **Staying in sync with FINDINGS-LOG.md**: every card and table ID must exist in the log, or the build fails. Every number in the prose is a
  `{template}` over data.json (no hand-typed numbers). Verification lines are pulled from `logs-summary.txt`, so when a
  fix lands and a test flips to PASS, the card says so. Planned: a lint that lists log entries with no table row, and table rows whose
  log entry has been marked RETRACTED or NEG since.
- Per-finding sketch code lives in build.py (`SKETCHES` registry). A new card needs an export script, a yaml entry
  and, only if no existing sketch type fits, a sketch function.
- Planned: a `make report` target and a CI job that runs the export and render and fails if a PASS/FAIL line changed without the card
  being updated (the diff shows up in build-manifest.json).

## 13. Build status (full report)

- **Layout of the source.** `findings.yaml` holds report metadata, the ranked table, short cards (`short:`) and the compact table
  (`compact:`). Each full card is `cards/<ID>.yaml`. Each sketch is `sketches/<name>.py` on the shared primitives in `sketchlib.py`.
  Each live panel is `templates/panels/<id>.js` on `templates/widgets/panel.js`. Live-path short cards are merged from `short-live.yaml`.
- **In-browser panels** now use the WASM line's toolchain: `wasm/build-variants.sh` compiles every variant in `wasm/variants.yaml` from `git archive`
  of the pinned SHA, with `harness/prep-src.sh`, the wasm line's size-flag patch, its C API and `tools/make-bundle.mjs`. The output is one classic-script bundle
  per variant (`prototype/wasm/audapter-<v>.js`, about 392 KB, works from `file://`) plus `<v>.build.json` recording the SHA, patch hashes and buffer sizes.
  There is one variant per finding (`fix-*`, and `alt-f6` for the F6 alternative), each on "lite" sizes: recorders 10 s, playback and delay buffers as shipped.
  `build.py` refuses a panel whose declared `needs` (e.g. `shipped_playback`, `recorder_s`) or patch list do not match the variant's build record.
- **Patch verification** (`wasm/check-patches.mjs`, per-variant `wasm/checks/<v>.mjs`) checks two things. First, each variant is bit-identical to the shipped
  build on every reference scenario in `audit/wasm/testdata` that the patch is not meant to change: audio, signalIn/Out, and the data matrix minus the LPC columns (WASM-1).
  Second, its target scenario shows the bug on shipped and not on the variant. Results are written to `wasm/check-patches.json`.
- **Links** to notes, the log and scripts point at the public repository (`report.repo_url`), with the anchors GitHub generates for each heading.
- **Live path**: the live-path line's verified results (LIVE-1 to LIVE-10) are in; the formal and corpus lines are placeholders until verified.
- **Publishing**: `audit/publish/stage.sh` copies `prototype/` to `/docs` and runs the public-information gate.

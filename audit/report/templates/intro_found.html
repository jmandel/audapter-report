The core works: formant shifts deliver what they log, and pitch shifts land within a few cents of what was asked.

No blab experiment we inspected is exposed to trial-to-trial carry-over. We checked the OST files and the way conditions are switched between trials in 10 public blab experiments and in blab's unpublished experiment code (including timeWrap, pitchAdaptPD and pitchComp). None uses the settings that let one trial's state leak into the next, and in simulated mixed sessions with the lab's settings, catch trials received no perturbation whatever came before them (details).

What does touch current experiments:

  • Masking noise drops out. In the many blab runners that load the noise once per session, since b2.4 (April 2026) the bundled babble goes silent for [[I01_gap_ms_min:.0f]] ms about every 10 s of running time: in about 1 in [[I01_one_in:.1f]] trials of a masking phase. Data collected before April 2026 are not affected (I-01).
  • A loudness and latency step in two time-warp studies. In timeWrap and cerebTimeAdapt the pre phase runs with the phase vocoder off and later phases in its time-warp mode, so from the first phase after pre onward participants hear their own voice about 3 dB quieter in level (a loudness model puts it at about 2.7 dB) and [[PT5_tw_lat_step:.0f]] ms later. The timeAdapt family is at about 0 dB (PT-5).
  • Formant shifts change the level. blab's MATLAB defaults switch off Audapter's gain adaptation, so a formant shift also changes the output level: on real voices, lowering F1 by 125 mel raised it by a median [[FMTLEVEL_dn125_g0_med:.0f]] dB and raising F1 lowered it by [[FMTLEVEL_up125_g0_med:.0f]] dB. Perceived loudness changes less (about 1.3–3 dB by a loudness model) but still enough to notice, so shifted and unshifted trials differ in loudness in every blab formant study (FMT-LEVEL).
  • vsaSentence depends on an undocumented blab change. The field perturbation switches on again at every vowel; on the original Audapter 2.1.5 or another fork, only the first vowel of each sentence would be perturbed (F6).
  • One open question about the public vsaGeneralize runner. As committed, it perturbs the baseline trials and never scales the field (LAB-2); its experiment script calls it, and the lab's logs can settle it. The later vsaGeneralize2 study is wired correctly. Two smaller notes: vsaCentralize's repository ships an unused, mis-wired runner (LAB-1), and vsaGeneralize's p.fb4Gain line has no effect, as the lab's code already notes (LAB-3).

Pitfalls for future designs. Carry-over is real with other settings: an offset rule placed after a timer or after the RMS-floor rule fires later on every trial (OST-F1); a PCF left loaded overrides a later field experiment (COORD-1); pitch catch trials made by clearing the PCF can be shifted (OST-F5). Each card gives the safe pattern. Other pitfalls include wrongly logged pitch for low voices with time-domain shifting, crashes or hangs when settings change mid-session, and clicks when resetting during speech (all).

Robustness. A few memory errors need a malformed file or array to show up (all).

None of these produce a warning. Most cards include a fix you can try on this page.
